Brett Williams

Brett is an accomplished and versatile librarian, researcher and open-source intelligence professional based in Toronto, with over a decade of experience across a broad range of library services and academic disciplines. He holds a Master of Library and Information Studies (MLIS) from McGill University and began his career establishing the library systems for the College of the North Atlantic-Qatar.

Publications

  • Naive Bibliometrics: The ongoing bibliometric impact of a racist article — #LD4:2026 Conference (Linked Data Conference 4), September 28, 2026. Four bibliometrics articles published in racial hereditarian journals form a natural experiment on data-source scope and the impact of racial hereditarian research; an outlier paper is the most highly cited paper for all its authors, showing a problem with naive use of citation counts as a measure of quality.
  • ALA Core Division Panel on International Librarianship — American Library Association, August 25, 2026. A panel discussion on academic libraries outside of North America.
  • Google Sheets for Arbitrary Metadata Transformations & Visualizations — Code 4 Lib North, December 3, 2025. Demonstration of tools inside and external to Google Sheets, including Timeline.js, Kumu, HighlightThis and OpenMultipleUrls, to manage mid-sized information projects and visualizations.
  • Terror on the Rise: What the increase in terrorism charges in Canada can tell us about the threat — Insight Monitor, January 8, 2025. On the rise of terrorism in Canada, illustrated by increasing attacks and charges and fueled by geopolitical tensions and growing radicalization across ideological lines.
  • Attack Patterns & Money Trails: Measuring Terrorist Financing — Insight Monitor, November 20, 2024. A new way of thinking about measuring terrorist financing, and a first step towards quantifying what that looks like in Canada.
  • Tireless, Enthusiastic, Naive: AI Search in the Health Library — Library A.I.nnovation Panel and Workshop, November 13, 2024. A panel discussion and workshop, with slides from the workshop, demonstrating Scite and Undermind.
  • Rising Threat: The Surge of Ideologically-Driven Terrorism in Canada — Insight Monitor, September 5, 2024. On the terrorism threat in Canada; since 2004 ideologically motivated violent extremists have been responsible for the majority of terrorist attacks in Canada and have killed three times the number of people killed by religiously motivated violent extremists.
  • RCMP Intelligence — Insight Monitor, November 30, 2023. A primer on RCMP Intelligence functions.
  • Visualizing the Impact of Academic Misconduct in the Health Sciences: The Citation Networks of Retracted Papers — BRIC 2023, June 8, 2023. Academic misconduct from data-collection mistakes to fraud is a major issue; using the Dipak Das retracted-papers case, it maps what misconduct looks like in citation networks after disclosure, using Dimensions and VOSViewer techniques.
  • Applied Bibleometric Mapping for Searching using Citation Gecko, Dimensions and VosViewer — BRIC 2022, June 15, 2022. Keynote building on the 2020 Code4Lib article, using Zotero, Citation Gecko, Dimensions and VosViewer to rapidly find relevant articles for complex searches, including a data block model and citation mining at scale.
  • Building a Research Portal as a part of an integrated workspace — OLA 2022, February 2, 2022.
  • Research, Scanning and Tool Building All in a Special Library? We've Got It "covid-ed"! — OLA 2021, February 3, 2021 (with Karen L.). Ontario Medical Association librarians on streamlining and integrating research, scanning and tool building, leveraging technology and modifying workflow, and customizing cloud-based research tools to address challenges presented during COVID-19.
  • Bibleometrics, Finding and Academic Misconduct — Dimensions and Altmetric Workshop, May 28, 2020.
  • Dimensions & VOSViewer Bibliometrics in the Reference Interview — Code 4 Lib Journal, February 17, 2020. VOSviewer bibliometric mapping using Dimensions, Scopus and Web of Science data; advanced searches in Dimensions, citation-data export, and using the resulting maps in reference interviews for graduate students, faculty, and post-PhD researchers.
  • What You Don't See — OLA Superconference 2019, February 1, 2019. Two academic librarians with overseas experience share examples of censored information and repression of free expression, and discuss how lost information can be unearthed.
  • Confessions of a Scan Artist — OLA Superconference 2019, January 31, 2019. Two librarians at the nexus of academic and special libraries on news monitoring in a highly political environment, with grey-literature tips, custom tools, and strategies for a rapidly changing information landscape.
  • How to Manage Libtech Innovation With Wardley Value Chain Mapping — Computers in Libraries, October 2017. Wardley value chain mapping as a simple, effective technology-planning tool, usable across organizations without extensive training.
  • Wardley Value Chain Mapping in Library Technology — Computers in Libraries 2017, March 2017. An introduction to Wardley Value Chain Mapping, with examples of how systems librarians, library administrators, and library IT decision makers can use it.
  • Reference in the Wild: Personal and Professional Use of Quora and Stack Exchange — OLA Superconference 2017, February 2, 2017. Question and answer websites as a powerful tool for building reference skills; an introduction to Quora and Stack Exchange, publication strategy, search strategies, and their unique cultures for research.
  • International Perspectives on Information Literacy: A Panel Discussion — Georgia International Information Literacy Conference, October 10, 2014 (with Kayo Chang Black). Three Canadian academic librarians — one in Canada, one in Hong Kong, and one in Qatar — on what information literacy competency and education means for them and their students.
  • "Officially Speaking": The Latter-day Saint Documentary Opposition to the Equal Rights Amendment — Research in Religious Studies Student Conference 2006, February 2006. An exposition and analysis of official statements from the Salt Lake City-based Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints opposing the proposed Equal Rights Amendment to the US constitution.
